# Business Memory System for Service Agencies

The Business Manager product started from a customer observation: "most of our customers are either service business or a consultative agency or creative studios. The number one pain point is always about what's going on in my business? How's it going with that client? And is that person going to close or are they going to churn?" [00:14]. CRMs should have solved this, "but in reality, I would say 70 to 80% of them have never used a CRM tool. Most of them are either tracking things just on their communication apps or in a Google Sheet" [00:30]. Sean's diagnosis is that "the problem is never about documenting the data because it's so easy to have data in one place, but those data will decay over time" [00:49] - the same decay problem that the memory-system design solves with gated consolidation into durable semantic facts. What the agencies want is "a memory system to enable every single business with an internal AI chief of staff or like having a Jarvis for your business" [00:59].

The product layers a memory system on top of a lead inbox. The Leads Hub lists all leads and documents past WhatsApp and email conversations - a pain that explodes when ads on Instagram or Facebook push a WhatsApp link and "the inbounds could just go sky rocketed over time" [01:35]. The direct inspiration was Claude Code: because the startup runs on Notion, GitHub, Linear Tickets, and Slack, "as a founder and CEO, all I had to do is that I literally just turn on Clock code and ask it what is going on in my startup" [01:58]. That produced a right-hand sidebar chat called the Business Manager [02:09]. In the demo, Sean picks Lucy, the lead with the highest total value, asks "how was the previous conversation with Lucy", sees her thread, then drafts "a follow-up message to book a time on Tuesday with Lucy" and sends it straight through WhatsApp from the sidebar [02:24]. The WhatsApp system connects to the company's AI features "where you can train your own agents" [03:08].

The second integration is CRM sync for the sizable companies that do use a CRM: "Currently, we have HubSpot connected, and I can just ask my business manager what leads are there in my HubSpot CRM" [03:37]. The manager reads the CRM, lists the leads, and pulls selected contacts in - "we might want to sync either the entire previous customer list or just the customers that already won so that we can enable these leads with our memory system and the business manager" [03:55]. This positions the Business Manager as an extension of the earlier WhatsApp messaging and leads tool: "Now, we're all in on building the memory system and a second brain for the business owners in consultative agencies and service industries" [04:15]. In memory-system terms, the Leads Hub and conversation logs are the episodic timeline, the synced CRM records and won deals are the durable semantic facts, and the chief-of-staff chat is the working memory that stitches them together so the business owner can ask questions without maintaining a CRM by hand.
