# AI Content Creation

AI Jason's channel is the cleanest case study in the corpus. Jason Zhou runs a Sydney-based channel with 222,000 YouTube subscribers, 30.5k on X, and a 1,281-member AI Builder Club [01:34]. The origin story is a joke that scaled: "I put Jian Yang's face on the thumbnail and that was the first video I got like really a blow up view" [00:56], and "50% of people I think went in the video because they really thought Jian Yang is making AI videos 3 years ago" [01:04]. He started the channel simply "to keep myself... have this momentum of learning" [05:16], with an earlier Kanye West AI-voice-clone experiment in Google Colab [05:44], and the first video landing May 4, 2023 [05:33].

His content philosophy is prototype-first, positioning against the "two type of content creators" - hyper-technical videos most people cannot stand, or hype-driven news where "everything is feel like oh my god the new king AGI" [06:14]. Jason's lane: "try some new things and then figure out how can I apply that" [06:28], build a proof of concept, and show viewers how to "not only understand concept but also know what they can do about it" [06:51]. Sean himself learned LangChain and LangGraph from Jason's videos [07:00]. The format defies the 5-minute playbook: depth won, "people get used to this 20, even 40, 1 hour videos" [08:21]. Editing a 30-minute video takes him about an hour [09:54], while AI video editing remains unsolved because video is three-dimensional information [10:36]. His one rule: "you basically just need to make useful things. All the other things are actually doesn't matter" [43:27].

The psychological product of this content is a cure for AI anxiety. "If you are in AI, every day you will feel like AGI is arrived and your business is going to die. But if you just take a break in the mountain for a month and you come back you realize everything is still the same" [36:41]. The real founder skill, credited to the PM of Claude Code, is "try to really understand the boundary of the model... design a product that take user to a golden path" [37:24]. Hands-on trial, not news consumption, is what dissolves the FOMO [09:05].

Content is also the distribution engine for AI startups. Sean pitches AutoManus with "about a million organic views and 45,000 followers across multiple channels" and notes "a lot of our customers actually inbound into my product because of my videos" [00:55], yielding "125 sign-ups from businesses with zero CAC" [04:33]. Jason's videos begat Superdesign's 4,000 GitHub stars [16:26], his US first round [16:44], and later Trulyt. In the Shenzhen event, Sean and the ex-Kimi product lead extend the playbook to Chinese sellers going global: "use content as the top of funnel" [30:52], accept that "perhaps 0.1% of viewers become actual buyers" [30:45], "showing up consistently. You gotta show up" [31:26], because "familiarity itself can sometimes drive purchases more effectively than highly technical content" [31:41]. Start "with an altruistic mindset... consider what shared knowledge your target audience wants to learn" [32:08]. The boundary: even AI-native marketing cannot fake the human - Maya argues AI "can't actually create that content that is authentic and what people actually really want to see and resonate with" [13:09].

The audience these channels serve is the emerging class of AI-native companies: builders with blurred roles who "can actually build really awesome things once they get into the builder role" [45:56]. Jason hires through the channel ("we are looking for... engineers" [46:29]), Sean's Shanghai meetup posted two days in advance drew 40+ people because "most Chinese people don't talk much on the internet, but they're like super hardcore" [48:36]. Content, in this corpus, is how AI founders recruit users, employees, and investors simultaneously.
