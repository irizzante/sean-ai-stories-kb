# UK Startup Ecosystem

The UK scene in this video is populated by young, non-technical, consumer-first founders: Maya Atso building [Rebeauty](/entities/maya-atso.md), "essentially Vinted, but for beauty" [01:36], and Victoria Peronnet Miller building [Ventureship](/entities/victoria-peronnet-miller.md), "basically hiring reinvented" [01:59]. Both are 24, both found Sean through TikTok [00:36], and both are hunting for technical co-founders - Maya is "extremely looking forward to finding... a CTO" [11:07] because "the technical side really isn't for me" [11:20]. Their hiring instinct is telling about the ecosystem: even AI-native founders still hire humans for authentic social content [12:56] and hire their own interns via take-home projects [13:29].

The dominant theme is the London-San Francisco gap. "America is so much more friendly and... much more startup friendly" [16:17], while UK culture "just likes to [take the mickey] on each other" [16:46] and resists people who do not fit "a kind of confined box that society wants you to fit into" [17:01]. Victoria's ten days in an SF accelerator felt humbling: "I don't know what they drink over there. They've got rocket fuel in their veins" [18:08], in a city where "everyone there's an investor" [18:19]. Her formula: "America's more in conviction rather than proof and the UK's more in proof because we're so risk averse" [19:06]. Sean's Antler London rejection - where partners demanded projected revenue from pre-seed teams [19:44] - versus US investors who called that same projection a red flag [20:03], makes the gap concrete. The UK's self-inflicted trap: "they want you to launch before they give you the money, but you need the money before you can launch" [22:31].

The ecosystem has real strengths. Britain offers "one of the best tax relief systems for investment in the world... SEIS or EIS" [23:01], where a £10,000 angel investment in a SEIS-approved startup returns 70% - "really 10,000 is only 3,000" [23:16]. Victoria calls the risk aversion irrational given that subsidy [22:55]. The female-founder infrastructure is growing fast: Barclays Eagle Labs, Female Founders Rise, and Let's Fund Her [23:43], against a backdrop where "in the UK there's very limited funding that goes to female founder companies" [24:06]. FFR's campaign put successful alumnae founders on billboards across the country under "we've done it, you can do this too" [24:45], which matters because "if you can't see yourself or someone like you in that position, you probably just automatically assume that it's out of reach" [25:31]. Sean extends the representation point with his own SF moment: "the moment when you land in San Francisco, you just realize that 50% of the people are Asians" [26:42], and "I was like, 'Shit, these people can do it, and I can do it, too'" [27:09].

Founder psychology is a live topic here, from imposter syndrome to advice hygiene. Maya quotes her dad: "the difference between a winner or a loser is a winner never gives up" [29:52]; Odd Muse founder Amy describes being "at the point where other people would have given up and that's why it feels so bad" [30:29]. Sean's imposter-syndrome cure: "you don't need to project other people's success or the potential... failure onto yourself" [32:11], and Naval's line that "when you do something that you feel like you're playing, you're winning" [32:46]. Victoria attacks perfectionism: "being messy is actually such an advantage cuz it means that you put yourself out there before you feel ready" [62:58], and "no one ever feels ready" [63:02]. Overrated advice is flagged too: "follow your heart" can trap you "in the local maximum for a very long time without even trying to find the global maximum" [61:49]; the better loop is "set a time limit... go all in, iterate as much as you can... and then move on" [64:12].

Outsider views corroborate the picture. Jason Zhou, from Australia, says "London actually has pretty good VC environment from what I understand" [14:41], while "overall in Europe, the VCs are still more conservative compared to the US" [14:54], because "Silicon Valley just have too many angels who made money from their previous acquirement or IPOs" [15:02]. Antler's own people told Sean "the best portfolio companies usually come from Australia, Singapore, and Scandinavian countries" [12:55] - English-speaking ecosystems that, like London, must sell to the US market from day one.
