# a16z partners are all in founder mode for their a16zspeedrun program

Sean received three invitation emails from a16z speedrun partners to apply to the latest cohort. One partner told him he was "at the top 5% of their cohort," while the other two did not mention it, so he joked on X that he would simply use the top-5% email to apply. The joke quickly drew the attention of the fund itself: "the partner at A16Z Andrew Chen messaged me on Twitter on X. Their marketing partner Ryan also messaged me" [00:40].

Rather than defending the campaign, the partners jumped straight into debugging with Sean. They asked which emails he had received, and together they figured out the cause: Sean had applied with one or two emails and had also signed up for their Substack mailing list with a couple of emails, which is why three invites landed. Sean frames this as "true founder mode" [01:21] and notes how unusual it is that "partners at um a fund like A16Z just messaging you and and trying to debug with you together" [01:12].

The lesson Sean draws is about customer engagement, not outreach volume. "This is what founders are supposed to do when your potential customers or existing customers are talking about your products publicly you should read them and engage with your customers" [01:31]. He acknowledges the partners are "really trying hard to push hard on acquiring the best founders in the world to apply" [01:41], even though some commenters on his tweet said they also got spammed. Overall, he concludes a16z is "doing a good job in terms of getting close to their customers" [02:00].
