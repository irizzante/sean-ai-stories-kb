# Leon Xiao Ming

Leon, introduced by Sean as "my friend Leon, Xiao Ming. Former product lead at Kimi. Currently raised tens of millions. Over $40 million" [01:34], builds two products: YouWare, "a vibe coding tool that appeared before Lovable" [01:45], and Bloome, "mainly for team collaboration, tools for invoking agents" [01:52]. He booked the Shenzhen coffee-shop venue for the interview himself [02:02], following earlier events in Beijing and Shanghai [02:31].

His career tracks the Chinese AI wave. He graduated in 2018 and came to Shenzhen to work on phones and hardware [03:37], joined ByteDance in 2020 on Jianying ("overseas, it's called CapCut" [03:46]), and left after '23 "when Midjourney and ChatGPT exploded" [03:53] and internal validation slowed AI bets [04:03]. He "spoke with Kimi's co-founders. We hit it off immediately... That was Kimi's darkest time" [04:08], and lived the reversal: "from being the last among large model companies to suddenly exploding in March 2024" [04:19]. Around July/August 2024, "AI coding capabilities became incredibly strong. A major turning point was Sonnet 3.5" [04:35], whose "most important ability was... enabling ordinary people to code" [04:45] - so "we resolutely left Kimi to start our own venture" [05:08]. A fan of Sean's channel "since the early days" [06:28], he initially "thought you were an ABC" [06:33].

His product philosophy is radical but concrete. "From day one with Bloome, we treated agents as people" [24:21]; agents need social graphs rather than a single owner [24:39], echoing Marx - "Man is the ensemble of social relations" [25:10]. Organizationally, "it essentially becomes permission management" [27:51], and "every company is essentially a file system" [29:10]. His own team now sends "nine out of ten messages @mention agents instead of people" [36:02], which "emerged organically" [36:11], for an estimated "at least a 5x improvement" [23:20]. He also brings the pricing argument: "token-based pricing is inherently flawed" [37:37], dock-worker piece-rate from his native Hubei [37:44], and "pricing must theoretically be outcome-based" [39:10], with trust platforms (Taobao escrow, "Stripe built a cult in Silicon Valley" [41:19]) and Anthropic's consulting-firm acquisitions [40:23] as the endgame.

He describes himself as "an introvert type" [17:20] who reflects independently, prioritizes "Radical Candor" [20:19], visits the US "every two or three months" [07:21], and repeats Su Hua's advice: "First build a castle, only then does a moat matter" [46:45].
